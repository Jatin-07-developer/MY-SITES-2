The Sun

At the heart of our solar system lies the Sun, a massive sphere of hot plasma that accounts for 99.86% of the
system's total mass. This incredible star provides the light and warmth necessary for life on Earth and governs
the motion of all planets through its immense gravitational pull.

The Eight Planets
Mercury
The smallest planet in our solar system and closest to the Sun, Mercury is a
rocky world with extreme temperature variations.
Distance from Sun 57.9 million km
Diameter 4,879 km
  • A year on Mercury is just 88 Earth days.
  • Surface temperatures range from -173°C to 427°C.
  • Has no atmosphere to retain heat
Venus
Named after the Roman goddess of love and beauty, Venus is the hottest
planet in our solar system despite not being the closest to the Sun.
Distance from Sun 108.2 million km
Diameter 12,104 km
  • Rotates in the opposite direction to most planets.
  • A day on Venus is longer than its year.
  • Thick atmosphere traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect.
Earth
Our home planet is the only known world to harbor life. Earth's atmosphere
and liquid water make it uniquely suited for life as we know it.
Distance from Sun 149.6 million km
Diameter 12,742 km
  • 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water.
  • The only planet not named after a god.
  • Has one natural satellite: the Moon.
Mars
Known as the Red Planet, Mars has fascinated scientists for centuries with its
potential for past microbial life and future human exploration.
Distance from Sun 227.9 million km
Diameter 6,779 km
  • Home to the largest volcano in the solar system: Olympus Mons.
  • Has two small moons: Phobos and Deimos.
  • Evidence suggests Mars once had liquid water on its surface.
Jupitor
The largest planet in our solar system, Jupiter is a gas giant with a mass
more than twice that of all other planets combined.
Distance from Sun 778.5 million km
Diameter 139,820 km
  • Has the Great Red Spot, a storm larger than Earth.
  • Has at least 95 known moons.
  • One day on Jupiter is only 10 hours long.
Saturn
Famous for its spectacular ring system, Saturn is the second-largest planet
and the least dense - it would float in water!
Distance from Sun 1.43 billion km
Diameter 116,460 km
  • Has the most spectacular ring system of any planet.
  • Has at least 146 known moons, including Titan.
  • Made mostly of hydrogen and helium.
Uranus
This ice giant rotates on its side, making it unique among the planets. Its
blue-green color comes from methane in its atmosphere.
Distance from Sun 2.87 billion km
Diameter 50,724 km
  • Rotates nearly on its side at 98 degrees.
  • Has 13 known rings and 27 known moons.
  • Coldest planetary atmosphere in the solar system.
Neptune
The farthest planet from the Sun, Neptune is a dynamic ice giant with the
strongest winds in the solar system.
Distance from Sun 4.50 billion km
Diameter 49,244 km
  • Has wind speeds up to 2,100 km/h.
  • Takes 165 Earth years to orbit the Sun.
  • Has 14 known moons, the largest being Triton.
Solar System Facts
  1. Our solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a giant rotating cloud of gas and dust.
  2. There are millions of asteroids in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
  3. The solar system extends far beyond Neptune, including the Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud.
  4. All planets orbit the Sun in the same direction and roughly the same plane.
  5. Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.
  6. The solar system is located in the Orion Arm of the Milky Way galaxy.
